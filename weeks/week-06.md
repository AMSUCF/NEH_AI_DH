This week takes the visual work outside the classroom. AI-generated images are everywhere — in your social feeds, in commercial advertising, in the patterns being sold on Etsy, in a Netflix episode you may have watched. Pick a community you spend time in and look hard.

## Exercise Menu

- <span class="tag tag-light">Light</span> **AI-detection scavenger hunt (~30 min).** Pick a community where visuals matter — a hobby forum, a subject area on Pinterest, an Instagram or TikTok subculture you actually use. Find three images that you suspect are AI-generated. Run them through Claude Sonnet for analysis ("is this image likely to be AI-generated, and what cues led you to that conclusion?"). Reflect on what cues helped you spot or miss them. *Source: `HumanitiesAI/weekeight.md`.*
- <span class="tag tag-standard">Standard</span> **Generate, then critique (~90 min).** Use Claude (or Adobe Firefly, Gemini, Midjourney trial) to generate three images relevant to your discipline. Document the iteration: what changed between attempts. Critique the results through one of this stage's readings — Demsky, Jebb, or Crawford & Paglen. *Source: `HumanitiesAI/weekfive.md`.*
- <span class="tag tag-deep">Deep</span> **Build a small visual archive Artifact (~3 hr).** Take a public-domain collection (LOC, Internet Archive). Upload 15+ images to a fresh Project. Generate accessible alt-text for each, build a thematic visualization, publish as an Artifact gallery. This is the closest async exercise to the spirit of Refik Anadol's *Archive Dreaming.*
